1. Assignment Overview & Objectives

This assignment implements an app-less, cross-platform mobile WebXR Augmented Reality experience and evaluates a 3D asset decimation and compression pipeline.

  • Objective: Deliver marker-based AR directly in mobile web browsers without installing native apps (Unity / APKs).
  • Target Course Outcomes: CO1 (Literature Survey & Architectural Trade-off Analysis) and CO2 (3D Asset Decimation & WebXR Deployment).
  • Core Technology: AR.js, A-Frame, Three.js, WebGL, glTF 2.0, and Draco Compression.

2. What We Made (System Overview)

Summary: We built an interactive WebAR web application that runs in standard mobile browsers (Safari on iOS, Chrome on Android, and PC webcams). When the user points their camera at a standard Hiro marker, a 3D Cyber Reconnaissance Drone automatically pops up floating and rotating above the marker with sound effects, telemetry HUD, and interactive energy reactions.
  • App-less Delivery: Zero installation required. Users simply open a public HTTPS link or scan a QR code.
  • Real-Time Marker Tracking: Uses WebAssembly ARToolKit to track the Hiro marker pattern via the device webcam at 60 FPS.
  • Dynamic Hover & Audio Reaction: When the marker is detected, the drone plays a lock-on sonar sound and begins a floating levitation animation.
  • Interactive Energy Shockwave: Clicking "⚡ React" triggers an expanding energy shockwave shield and bursts the rotor speed by 8×.
  • Wireframe & Switcher Controls: Evaluators can toggle wireframe mode or switch between Low-Poly and High-Poly models live in AR.

4. What is Our 3D Model?

We designed a multi-component Cyber Reconnaissance Drone (Model CRD-07) specifically tailored for augmented reality visualization:

  • Central Fuselage & Hull: Aerodynamic core housing flight avionics and navigational sensors.
  • Glowing Reactor Core: Emissive cyan spherical reactor located at the center of the drone.
  • Magnetic Containment Ring: Outer toroidal stabilization ring wrapped with electromagnetic coils.
  • Quad Propulsion Pylons: 4 diagonal structural arms extending outward to the motor housings.
  • Turbine Engine Nacelles: 4 aerodynamic duct cowlings with rotating turbine blades.
  • Optical Sensor Turret: Front-mounted gimbal turret with dual infrared optical camera lenses.
  • Cooling & Armor: Segmented top armor plating, aerodynamic stabilizer fins, and rear copper conduits.

5. How We Optimized It (Part B: Blender Pipeline)

To run smoothly at 60 FPS on mobile phones without lagging or draining battery, we applied a 5-step optimization pipeline:

  • Step 1: High-Poly Baseline Authoring: Created the high-poly sculpt containing 116,744 triangles, 65,760 vertices, and 10 separate sub-meshes (10 draw calls), resulting in a heavy 2.95 MB file size.
  • Step 2: Mesh Decimation (Quadric Error Metric): Applied Blender's Decimate Modifier in Collapse mode with a ratio of 0.021. This collapsed redundant coplanar vertices while preserving silhouettes, reducing triangles by 97.86% down to 2,504 triangles.
  • Step 3: Draw Call Consolidation: In WebGL, each separate material causes a laggy draw call. We merged 8 structural parts into a single hull mesh, reducing draw calls from 10 down to 2 (an 80.00% reduction).
  • Step 4: Normal Texture Baking: Baked fine surface details (panel lines, bevels, conduit ribs, bolt rivets) from the high-poly model into a tangent-space normal map to keep visual sharpness without polygon overhead.
  • Step 5: Draco Geometry Compression: Exported the final asset with Draco compression (`KHR_draco_mesh_compression`), compressing the geometry down to just 15.8 KB (0.015 MB)!
